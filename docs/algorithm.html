How Colors works

A palette generator for interfaces. Every palette is drawn from a statistical model of hand-picked editorial palettes; plates, gradients, interfaces, scales and SVGs are derived from it by rules. This page lists those rules, and marks where a rule is a plain consequence of the approach and where a special case steps in.

1 · Inputs

  • Data309 palettes (data/palettes.json), each with main, its light and dark tint, and two accents. A third of the colors are moved slightly (ΔE ≈ 1) so no palette is a copy; the statistics are unchanged.
  • Data36 near-whites and near-blacks (data/neutrals.json): what to use instead of pure white and black.
  • DataEnglish basic color terms (Many Languages, Many Colors) name the hue families.
  • RuleColor space: everything is computed in OKLab / OKLCH, inside the sRGB gamut. "Relative chroma" is chroma as a share of the most the gamut allows at that lightness and hue; the cusp is the most colorful color a hue can have at all.
  • RuleSeeds: every palette comes from a seed; separate random streams (palette, mood, gradient) keep one setting from changing the others.

2 · The palette

Five roles: main, companion, anchor, and the main's light and dark tint.

  • DataLightness and relative chroma of main, companion and anchor come from a Gaussian copula: the measured distribution of each value plus their correlations. Locked colors are fixed and the rest is drawn conditionally.
  • DataHues: the main's hue follows the dataset's hue preference at its lightness (light → yellows, dark → blues and reds); the others follow the measured hue offsets from the main, which are close to random.
  • DataTints: light and dark are the main, moved toward white or black by measured amounts, with slightly less chroma.
  • ExceptionStructural rejections: palettes with two or more neutrals, or with an anchor that is not the lightness pole, are redrawn with a probability (0.35 / 0.4 acceptance). The copula alone produces them more often than the data.
  • ExceptionCream anchors: a very light, nearly neutral anchor leans to cream with the measured probability. The data shows it; the copula has no hue.
  • DataLimits: chroma at most the palettes' 99th percentile (≈ 0.24); no pure black or white — near-neutrals take the lightness range and tint of the sampled near-blacks / near-whites (cool darks, warm lights).

3 · Mood (Muted ↔ Vivid)

A shift that stays inside the dataset; 0 is the data. The app no longer has the slider: new palettes are as in the data, and only old links with a mood still use it.

  • RuleWithin the distribution: each color's relative chroma is moved by shifting the normal score it was drawn with. Muted reaches the data's least vivid palettes (not greyer), vivid its most vivid ones.
  • RuleContinuous: the mood is applied to a palette drawn without it, so a change of the mood never draws new colors; canvases and plate shapes stay.
  • DataAt most two vivid colors: toward vivid only one accent (two with probability 0.4) moves toward its cusp; the others move down a little. The choice is fixed per palette.
  • TunedShift sizes: muted 1.1 σ, accents 2.5 σ, held back 1 σ.
  • ExceptionMuted widens contrast: toward muted the lightness range spreads by up to 20 %. The data's least vivid palettes make up with contrast.
  • ExceptionNeon above the cap: at the vivid end the accents may reach chroma 0.32, above the 0.24 limit. Otherwise they never look neon.

Neon Splash

A checkbox: the palette stays as it is, only one color becomes a neon.

  • RuleWhat neon is: the color's hue at full strength — the most colorful point that hue has in sRGB (its cusp), e.g. #DEFE00, #FE0072, #C000FE.
  • RuleWhich color: the free color that is closest to its own neon (OKLab distance), so the change is the smallest; a chosen hue family first. Locked colors stay.
  • DataNeon hues: the hues where the data has neons at least as often as on average (the measured share per 30° bin, interpolated): red-pink through orange, yellow and lime to green (≈ 3°–147°).
  • ExceptionHot pink from 350°: the neon hues start at 350° on the pink side. The data's bins are too coarse there; the cusps below 350° are a violet magenta that looked off.
  • TunedHues near them move in: a hue up to 40° outside moves to the nearest neon hue first, so a magenta becomes a hot pink (#FF03A9) and a teal a neon green. Blues and violets are too far and get no neon; if no color has one (≈ 4 % of palettes), nothing changes.
  • TunedLightness counts double in that distance: a pale green that only needs color wins over a mid pink that would also need to get lighter. A change of lightness changes the palette's structure; more color does not.
  • RuleSame composition: the fill colors of plate, gradient and interface are chosen without neon, so turning it on only changes colors.

4 · Hue families

  • RuleFamilies: every frequent one-word basic color term that is clearly colored (C ≥ 0.1) and at least 20° from a more frequent one: red, orange, yellow, green, teal, blue, purple, pink.
  • RuleOne hue: palettes are drawn until one with a clearly colored main, companion or anchor of that family appears (rejection sampling), so any role can carry it.
  • RuleTwo hues: every clearly colored color is of one of them, both appear. The same hue twice: monochrome. Neutrals are allowed.
  • ExceptionForced hue: if 1500 draws find nothing (rare, e.g. with locks), free colors are turned to the family. Then the palette is no longer purely from the model.

5 · Canvases and neutrals

  • DataPaper and ink (light and dark canvas) are drawn from the palettes' near-whites / near-blacks and the sampled ones.
  • ExceptionTint from the palette: a cream, cool or warm tint takes the nearest hue of a colored palette color. A cream paper next to purple and red looked khaki.
  • ExceptionLighter paper: paper lightness is mapped from the measured span (0.9–1) into 0.96–0.99. The measured near-whites are too dark for an app background.
  • RuleNeutral scale: from ink to paper, the tint interpolated in OKLab, so a cool ink and a warm paper meet in grey, not olive.
  • TunedNeutral chroma at most 0.015: the scale keeps only a hint of the canvases' tint. A canvas tinted by the companion made the neutral scale a copy of the companion's scale.

6 · Scales 50–950

  • RuleLightness curve: fixed per step, closer together at the light end.
  • RuleWhere a color sits: at the step of its lightness, but a colorful color needs paler steps above it: at least 1 + 2 × (chroma / cusp chroma) steps. So a vivid lime sits at 300, a trace of cyan at 100. The lightness curve bends around the color.
  • ExceptionNever 50: a palette color never sits at 50; 50 stays the near-white above it.
  • DataChroma profile: full in the middle, falling off toward white and gently toward black.
  • ExceptionPale colors: the profile is not taken literally for very pale colors (floor 0.12). A trace of color says little about how colorful its family may get; without it the scale turned grey.
  • RuleHue drift: a color that darkens turns toward the neighbouring hue whose cusp is darker (yellow → orange → brown, blue → indigo), lighter steps the other way. The amount follows the slope of the cusp lightness over hue.
  • TunedDrift weighting: the drift is weighted by how high the cusp is (from 0.57, full at 0.74), capped at 90° per unit of lightness. Fitted to the old measured drifts; it keeps purple from turning blue and lets orange darken to red-brown.
  • ExceptionPalette pull: below L 0.6 the steps lean toward the palette's darkest core color (red → plum next to a purple) — only if that color is dark (L < 0.5) and its hue is near (full up to 60°, none from 120°). Across the color circle the mix passes through grey.

7 · Colors 2 / 3 / 4

One setting, one color set, used by every view.

  • RuleThe set: the main plus 1, 2 or 3 fill colors, chosen from the palette as shown (with the mood). Plate, gradient, interface and SVG all use this set.
  • RuleFill order: the other palette colors first, the more colorful first; the main's own tints only after them. A fill must stand out from the main (lightness contrast ≥ 1.35 or OKLab distance ≥ 0.15) and from the other fills.

8 · Plates

  • RuleShape: one tile shape per plate, drawn from the seed; it stays when the mood moves or the color count changes, unless it has too few parts.
  • RuleColoring: pieces largest first, each takes the least used fill color its neighbours don't have.
  • ExceptionHand-picked splits: one-piece shapes are quartered or cut into bands; some shapes are excluded from cutting, rings of circles are rebuilt as overlapping circles. Decided shape by shape.

9 · Gradient

One fixed composition: a dark band on top with a soft tilted edge, a glow above a soft U-shaped edge, a light field below. Drawn as two radial CSS gradients.

  • TunedComposition: eight constants, measured from reference gradients and then varied by eye (mirrored, lower, flatter U, curved band).
  • RuleGlow: the most colorful color of the set that stands out (C ≥ 0.12, or 0.12 lighter than the darkest colored color).
  • RuleBand: a dark colored color, field: the lightest of the rest.
  • ExceptionNo near-black band when a dark color with body (L 0.22–0.6) is there. A deep purple over a lilac glow looks better than black.
  • ExceptionNeon field: a neon that is the lightest color by far becomes the field — but only if another vivid color (C ≥ 0.12) can glow above it and a third colored color can be the band. Else the neon glows. Otherwise a dull dark color would glow, or the neon would be band and field at once.
  • Rule2 colors: the more colorful one glows; the other is band or field; the missing stop is a deep or pale step of the glow's scale. 3: exactly the three. 4: the color left over turns the field into a second field toward the right.

10 · Interface tokens

Light and dark background

  • RuleLight: neutral-100 with white cards.
  • ExceptionA light near-neutral palette color (C < 0.03, L 0.84–0.98) becomes the background instead, at most L 0.955, with cards near white in its hue. A light neon shows on grey, not on white (Lime on Spare White).
  • RuleDark: a deep version (L 0.2) of the darkest colored color the interface uses; without one, neutral-950. Cards a little lighter.
  • ExceptionMain wins ties: a color within 0.1 lightness of the main counts as a tie, and the main gives the dark background.

Greys

  • RuleAll greys come from the neutral scale: text 950 / 50, muted text 700 / 400 (at least 4.5:1), light borders 200. Light text on colors is neutral-50. The canvases themselves stay paper and ink: the SVG backgrounds and brand-paper / brand-ink.

Colors

  • RuleThe loud color is the most colorful color of the set. It appears only in small fills: main action, logo, badge, today's bar, toggle track — always as itself, never darkened.
  • RuleBackground counts as a color: with 2, the interface is the loud color on neutrals; with 3 and 4, one or two more colors become the chart's data series. Tags, avatar and the secondary button stay neutral.
  • RuleData series without a second color: the loud color's own tint (two steps away on its scale).
  • ExceptionText on the loud color by APCA: light or a dark shade of the second color's hue, whichever reads better by APCA. WCAG 2 favors dark text on mid reds, though light reads far better.
  • ExceptionNav pill: the main's own light tint when the loud color is the main (as in the gradient), else step 100 of the loud color; the same in both modes.
  • RuleContrast: other colored tokens take the nearest step of their scale with 3:1 against the surface.

11 · Your SVG

  • RuleColors found after CSS and inheritance (fills, strokes, gradient stops; masks and clip paths left alone), ranked by area.
  • RuleMapping: the largest color takes the main, the others the set's fill colors; larger shapes get the fill with more lightness contrast, small ones may take a vivid color with little contrast (the dot).
  • RuleManual choices per color override the automatic one.
